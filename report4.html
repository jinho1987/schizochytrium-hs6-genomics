02

Introns & UTRs for intron-mediated enhancement (IME)

A native intron sitting inside the 5′UTR (or very early in the CDS) of a highly-expressed gene is one of the more reliable, underused synthetic-biology parts — splicing deposits an Exon Junction Complex at the splice site that improves nuclear export, translation initiation, and mRNA stability. It's a well-documented effect (maize Ubi1, rice Act1 are the classic examples) that's largely independent of the promoter itself.

StringTie-assembled transcript structures (merged across all 6 RNA-seq libraries) were matched to BRAKER CDS coordinates for 9,068 of the 9,236 stable-expression candidate genes, and searched for exon-exon junctions falling entirely inside the inferred 5′UTR.

False positives caught and removed

The initial scan returned several genes with implausible 12–14 kb "5′UTRs" — almost certainly StringTie merging two adjacent genes into one assembled transcript (read-through/fusion), not real UTR structure. These were filtered out (5′UTR length capped at a generous 1.2 kb) before reporting candidates below.

04

GO/KEGG enrichment (formal statistical test)

Formal hypergeometric enrichment testing (with FDR correction) on the HS6-vs-#4 significant DE gene set — this had already been run earlier in the project but is included here for completeness since it directly strengthens the interpretation used throughout this report series.

Headline result

The actin/myosin/cytoskeleton signature among genes down in HS6 is extremely significant: GO:0051015 (actin filament binding), 30/291 genes, p.adjust = 1.7×10⁻¹³. This puts real statistical weight behind the "less cytoskeleton, faster growth" interpretation of the HS6 phenotype — not just a descriptive impression from eyeballing gene lists.

Genes up in HS6 show much weaker enrichment (best p.adjust ≈ 0.026: ferric-chelate reductase activity, high-light-intensity response) — a real but modest signal, not comparable in strength to the cytoskeleton finding.

Targeted check: carotenoid-pathway genes, independent of DE status

The overlay above only looks near genes that are also significantly DE — which would miss a target-site resistance mutation that changes protein function without changing expression. HS6 was selected using EMS mutagenesis plus norflurazon, a herbicide that blocks the carotenoid-desaturation step; classic norflurazon resistance is often a point mutation directly in the desaturase gene itself. Pulled every HS6 gene mapped to the KEGG carotenoid-biosynthesis pathway (ko00906) plus every gene independently annotated (SwissProt + eggNOG) with a carotenoid-pathway function, and checked all 8 against the full 84-variant set directly — regardless of whether they were DE.

Two real caveats keep this from being a clean "ruled out": (1) the 84-variant set is itself a small, strictly-QC'd subset — a true causal variant could exist but have failed the confidence filter; (2) thraustochytrids like Schizochytrium are poorly represented in KEGG's orthology database for this pathway (only 2 of 63 possible carotenoid-pathway KOs had an HS6 gene at all), so a real desaturase-family gene could exist in the genome without being captured by either the KO-based or text-based search used here. The practical read: whatever conferred norflurazon resistance in HS6, it does not appear to be a simple coding or promoter-proximal mutation in the pathway's core annotated enzymes — resistance via a transporter, detoxification route, or a gene this search missed remains open.

One clear qualitative difference: DNA transposons

Strain #7 carries 134 DNA-transposon copies (mostly PiggyBac-family, 178.6 kb total) that are completely absent from HS6 (0 copies). HS6 in turn carries proportionally about 3× more LTR/Ty1-Copia retrotransposon content than #7 (0.26% vs 0.09% of the genome). Both patterns are consistent with the two strains having diverged TE landscapes despite being derived from the same lineage — but de novo family discovery on two genomes assembled and annotated independently can also produce this kind of asymmetry as an artifact of assembly completeness/fragmentation rather than real biology, so it's reported as an observation, not a mechanism.

No TE found at the MVK duplication breakpoint

Checked directly: within ±5 kb of the duplicated MVK locus (g10519, Scaffolds_28:710,505–712,837), the only annotated repeat features are a short unclassified element ~4.4 kb upstream of the gene start and a simple (CAAG)n repeat ~800 bp upstream — neither overlaps the gene body or sits at a plausible breakpoint position. This analysis does not support a TE-mediated mechanism for the Scaffolds_28 MVK duplication; segmental/whole-region duplication (e.g. via replication slippage or an unequal crossover with no TE involvement) remains the more likely explanation given the data on hand. Reported as a negative result rather than omitted, since the original motivation for running this analysis was explicitly to test this hypothesis.

RNA-seq-guided scaffolding

Applied the same Rascaf scaffolding approach used successfully in the sibling Chlorella HS2×ALE project (327→288 contigs, N50 +41% there) to HS6, using the existing 6 RNA-seq libraries — no new sequencing needed. Re-scoring telomere/chromosome completeness after scaffolding is the main payoff: some of HS6's 14 currently-partial scaffolds may resolve to complete chromosomes.

Stopped by request before completion. The BAM merge/sort finished, but the core rascaf alignment-support step was still running after ~2 hours of wall-clock time (an I/O-bound step on this drive) when it was terminated, and rascaf-join (the step that actually produces the merged-scaffold FASTA) never started. No scaffold-count or N50 result to report — the current 34-scaffold HS6 assembly, already used throughout every other analysis in this project, stands as-is.

Expression-constrained flux model (crude, by design)

Built a draft genome-scale metabolic model directly from the HS6 proteome using CarveMe (homology-based reconstruction against the BiGG universal reaction database), then applied a simple E-Flux-style constraint (each reaction's flux bound scaled by its associated gene's expression level) using the normalized RNA-seq counts, separately for WT4 and HS6.

Read this before the numbers below

This is exactly the "however crude it is" version explicitly asked for. CarveMe's default draft model used a generic, BiGG-derived biomass equation not calibrated to Schizochytrium — it literally included a bacterial peptidoglycan cell-wall precursor (uaagmda_c), which a eukaryotic alga simply doesn't have, and essentially zero lipid weighting despite this being a lipid-accumulation organism (50–77% dry-weight lipid reported in the literature under lipid-accumulation conditions). That's now corrected below: peptidoglycan removed, amino-acid coefficients reduced 45%, and a fatty-acid biomass term added (palmitate-weighted, matching typical thraustochytrid storage-lipid composition — true DHA (C22:6) has no metabolite ID in this generic reconstruction database, a real limitation noted rather than hidden).

Revised interpretation — the biomass fix wasn't the actual problem

Fixing the biomass equation left the WT4:HS6 ratio essentially unchanged (~2.1× either way). That's an important, honest correction to the caveat above: the counter-intuitive "HS6 lower than WT4" ordering is not mainly a biomass-equation artifact after all — it more likely reflects a real limitation of this crude E-Flux implementation itself, which caps each reaction's flux at a value scaled by the single highest-expressed gene among its associated genes. That kind of hard, per-reaction ceiling is known to behave unevenly when two conditions have different overall expression-distribution shapes, independent of real metabolic capacity. Net effect: don't trust the absolute growth-objective comparison between strains from this method, with or without the biomass fix. The qualitative pattern — central glycolysis dominates flux capacity in both conditions, with the specific downstream branch differing (glutamate dehydrogenase vs. glycerophosphodiester metabolism) — is the only part worth treating as a weak signal.

A properly useful version of this analysis would need: (1) a real Schizochytrium biomass equation from measured composition data, not a literature-typical approximation, (2) curation of the lipid/DHA-synthesis pathway reactions, which CarveMe's generic bacterial-leaning reaction database does not represent well for a PKS-type DHA synthase system, and (3) a more sophisticated flux-constraint method than linear E-Flux (e.g. GIMME or iMAT, which handle cross-condition expression-scale differences more carefully).

DEG statistical rigor & annotation audit (HS6 vs. #4)

The genotype-effect DE test (1,223 significant genes at padj<0.05, out of 13,529 tested) has been used throughout this project's reports. This section runs the standard rigor checks that hadn't been done yet: is the test itself well-calibrated, how complete is the annotation of what came out significant, what does the effect-size distribution actually look like, and does a proper KEGG pathway-level test back up the lipid-pathway narrative used elsewhere.

P-value calibration

A well-behaved DE test should show a roughly flat (uniform) p-value distribution among non-significant genes, with an excess concentrated near p=0 reflecting real signal. Binning all 13,529 p-values into deciles:

2.03×
enrichment in [0.0–0.1) bin vs. uniform-null
CV=0.042

Test is well-calibrated

The tail bins (p>0.5, where only non-significant genes should live) are nearly perfectly flat — bin counts range 1,116–1,345 against an expected ~1,253 under a uniform null, a coefficient of variation of just 0.042. That's the signature of a properly-calibrated test, not one artificially inflating significance. The 2.03× enrichment near p=0 is real but modest — consistent with a genuine but not overwhelming biological signal, which is exactly what you'd expect from an n=1 design: real effects are detectable, but the design can't support claiming a huge fraction of the genome is confidently affected.

Read the two rows very differently — confidence at the mutation site changes what the number means

g6423 (R27F): the mutation site itself is predicted with very high confidence in both alleles (pLDDT 96–97), so the 4.0Å local Cα shift there is on reasonably solid ground — plausible for an Arg→Phe substitution (long charged sidechain to bulky aromatic hydrophobic, BLOSUM62=−3, Grantham=205, the single most radical change in the whole missense survey). The large whole-chain RMSD (9.9Å, driven mostly by one region near residue 287) is a separate story: ESMFold is a single-sequence model with no multiple-sequence-alignment/coevolution signal, and LRR-superfamily proteins (this gene's family, §14) are elongated, repeat-unit structures whose overall curvature is often poorly constrained without one — that global number is most likely folding uncertainty, not a mutation effect, and shouldn't be read as "the mutation reshaped the whole protein."

g11453 (D511A): the opposite situation — the mutation site itself falls in a low-confidence region (pLDDT 40–41, below the conventional 50 cutoff where AlphaFold-family models stop making a confident local structure claim at all, consistent with this being an unannotated, possibly intrinsically disordered region). The 6.3Å local shift there is not reliable evidence of a real conformational change — it's equally consistent with ESMFold simply not having a confident answer for that stretch in either allele. The honest conclusion for this site is that ESMFold is inconclusive, not negative or positive; the Sanger primers in §14 remain the trustworthy way to confirm this mutation is real, and any structural claim about it would need a different method (e.g. a proper MSA-based model, or experimental structure work) to be credible.

Structural variant survey

Every mutation discussed elsewhere in this project — the qPCR panel (§13), the genome-wide missense survey (§14) — comes from exactly one small-variant caller (bcftools mpileup/call), which found 84 variants genome-wide between WT4 and HS6: 49 SNPs and 35 small indels (none in coding regions — confirmed by direct intersection with the CDS annotation). That number is strikingly small for two lines presumed to differ by a real, phenotype-driving mutation, and small-variant callers cannot see structural variants (SVs) at all — multi-kb deletions, duplications, inversions, translocations. They were never looked for in this project until now. This section runs a dedicated SV caller (Delly, germline mode) against the same WT4 resequencing BAM already used for the SNP/indel analysis.

Read this before the numbers below: draft, scaffold-level assembly changes how to interpret SV calls

The HS6 reference is a scaffold-level draft assembly, not a chromosome-level one. Discordant read-pair signals between different scaffolds (delly's BND/translocation calls) are the classic signature of either a real interchromosomal rearrangement or a scaffold ordering/orientation artifact in the assembly itself — the two are not distinguishable from short-read data alone. Delly found 455 raw BND calls (98 passing its own quality filter), heavily outnumbering every other SV type combined. These are reported for completeness but are not individually interpreted as confirmed rearrangements below — that would overclaim what scaffold-level short-read data can actually support. The intra-scaffold calls (DEL/DUP/INV, particularly the PRECISE/split-read-confirmed ones) are on much firmer ground and are the focus of this section.

Headline finding: a 421kb inversion with the strongest read support of any call in the dataset

Scaffolds_20:875,627–1,296,664 — 646 discordant read pairs agree

Whether this reflects a true biological inversion in HS6 or an assembly-ordering difference in how this region of the draft genome was scaffolded cannot be resolved from this data alone — the junction PCR below is designed specifically to answer that.

Known tool-install gotchas hit during this work (documented so they don't cost you the same time)

  • RepeatModeler uses -pa for parallel jobs, not -threads — the wrong flag causes it to print usage and silently produce no output, which then makes RepeatMasker fail downstream with a confusing "database does not exist" error.
  • eggNOG-mapper's download_eggnog_data.py hardcodes a dead domain (eggnogdb.embl.de) in two of its three download URLs; the working domain is eggnog5.embl.de (already used correctly for the third URL in the same file). Patch with: sed -i 's|eggnogdb.embl.de|eggnog5.embl.de|g' download_eggnog_data.py.
  • bioconda's eggnog-mapper package resolves to an ancient 2.0.1 build (python2.7, no --itype flag) unless pinned; pinning an exact recent version then conflicts with biopython in the conda solver. Installing via pip install eggnog-mapper==2.1.13 into a conda env with just diamond+hmmer sidesteps both problems.
  • CarveMe's model gene IDs are SBML-sanitized (g10740_t1, underscore not dot) — strip the _t\d+ suffix before matching against a plain gene-ID expression table, or every E-Flux constraint silently evaluates to zero and the model collapses to near-zero growth for every condition identically.
