Schizochytrium · DNA methylation & chromatin

Epigenetics: one methylome exists, and it is not HS6’s

Strain #7 has native PacBio 5mC calls. HS6’s sequencing delivery did not include them, and its parent #4 was sequenced on short reads only. This page records what the existing annotation and expression data already say about methylation and chromatin machinery, and which measurement would settle the open questions.

2 · DNA-methylation machinery

A thin toolkit for DNA methylation: one candidate C5 writer, no TET, UHRF or CXXC

The eggNOG-mapper annotation was scanned by exact Pfam family name. It annotates 9,134 of the 16,088 gene models (56.8%), so “not detected” below means absent from the annotated subset, not proven absent from the genome.

Readers and erasers

  • Not detected: SAD_SRA (UHRF/VIM-type 5mC reader), zf-CXXC and Tet_JBP (TET-type oxidase). The same scan finds a BAH-domain gene (g3653, ORC1) and a DMAP1 gene (g14076, a DNMT1-associated remodeling-complex subunit), but neither carries a methyltransferase domain.
  • PWWP: 5 genes (g604, g1397, g1597, g1825, g13194), none with a C5-methyltransferase domain, so there is no DNMT3-type candidate.
  • MBD: 2 genes (g696, g6296). Both also carry Bromodomain and WSD domains, which looks like a BAZ2/TIP5-type remodeler subunit more than a dedicated methyl-CpG reader.
  • Glycosylases (HhH-GPD): 4 genes (g7973 NTH1, g14967 OGG1-type, g14417 MAG1-type, g8861 EndoIII-like). These are canonical base-excision enzymes; no DEMETER/ROS1-type demethylating glycosylase was seen.
  • AlkB family (2OG-FeII_Oxy_2): 7 genes, including ALKBH1 (g13707). Possible erasers of 6mA or RNA marks in other systems; nothing here tests that.
What the gene content suggests

One candidate C5 writer and no TET/UHRF/CXXC would be consistent with a modest 5mC layer rather than a dense plant- or vertebrate-style methylome, and the MT-A70 family leaves room for 6mA or RNA m6A. This is a prediction from gene content. The #7 calls can test it directly.

4 · Expression in HS6 vs #4

No global change in the methylation or chromatin machinery

The 152 machinery genes above were joined to the HS6-vs-#4 table (adjusted for time; positive log2FC = higher in HS6). 137 have expression statistics and 8 are significant at padj<0.05 (5.8%), against 12.1% genome-wide (1,223 of 10,119 genes with an adjusted p-value), where about 16.6 would be expected by chance. The set is not enriched (hypergeometric P(≥8) = 0.995). The two candidate writers are unchanged: g1774 log2FC +0.20 (padj 0.91) and g11031 log2FC −0.04 (padj 0.98).

5 · Open questions elsewhere on this site

Where a chromatin or methylation measurement could help

  • A coordinated tandem-locus change (Extended Analyses). g6124 is down in HS6 and its unmutated tandem neighbour trends the same way. The report reads this as a locus-wide change, possibly through a shared chromatin or regulatory mechanism spanning both genes, and notes that the validated point variants near g6124 may be a marker rather than the sole cause. That reading has not been tested.
  • Candidate variants linked to g8342 and g8869. The same report says that, because both genes are unannotated, a regulatory role is speculative until a motif or a chromatin mark is found nearby.
  • The 421 kb inversion (g6674–g6776, 103 genes). Report 4 tests whether it dysregulates the genes inside it: 9 of 82 genes with statistics are significant (11.0%) against 9.0% elsewhere, Fisher p = 0.56. That gives no support for a region-wide chromatin effect from the inversion, though 82 genes is a small sample. Report 4’s baseline (1,214 of 13,447 genes outside the inversion) uses a different denominator from section 4 above (1,223 of 10,119 genes with an adjusted p-value); each is stated where it is used.
6 · What would settle it

Next steps, cheapest first

  1. Find and summarise the #7 call set. Mean CpG methylation, split by gene body, promoter and repeat or transposable-element sequence, strand symmetry, and CpG observed/expected in the #7 assembly. This needs no new sequencing and says whether 5mC is a substantial layer at all.
  2. Ask the sequencing provider for HS6’s original HiFi BAM with base-modification tags (MM/ML). PacBio’s on-instrument 5mC calls are stored in those BAM tags and are lost when reads are exported as FASTQ or FASTA, so converted files cannot be used. If the run did not record modification calls, they may not be recoverable and the reads would need re-sequencing. HS6 vs #7 then only makes sense in alignable regions.
  3. Add #4 for the comparison that matters. Long-read 5mC data for #4, with at least three biological replicates per strain at matched stages. Replication is what makes a differential-methylation call interpretable.
  4. Finish the machinery census on the full proteome. Search all 16,088 predicted proteins, and #7’s, for DNA_methylase, MT-A70, Tet_JBP, SAD_SRA and zf-CXXC. The eggNOG pass covers only 56.8%. This needs the protein FASTA, which is not in the repository.
  5. Histone-level assays at the g6124 locus. CUT&Tag or ChIP for marks chosen after checking which writer families are present, or ATAC-seq, on HS6 vs #4.
  6. 6mA is a separate question. The MT-A70 family is present, but PacBio’s default on-instrument modification calls typically cover 5mC at CpG, not 6mA. A 6mA question needs a dedicated approach.
