Schizochytrium sp. · Environmental Screening & Image Analysis

Finding Schizochytrium in the wild, by eye — then asking a computer to do it

Two questions, both open when this started: (1) across 60 environmental sample plates, which colonies actually look like Schizochytrium, ranked against 5 lab-maintained reference strains (HS6, #4, #7, M42, M64)? (2) can those 5 reference strains be visually told apart from each other at all? A from-scratch image-processing pipeline (dish detection, colony segmentation, isolation filtering) was built and iteratively corrected to answer both — this page documents the pipeline as much as the results, because for this dataset the two are inseparable.

This is an exploratory, crude-by-design pass, not a validated classifier. The reference data is thin (mostly one physical plate per strain, several dominated by lawn growth rather than isolated colonies), the "likeness" score is a similarity distance against a small pooled reference sample, not a trained/cross-validated model, and one environmental plate produced confirmed false positives from background noise.

A leave-one-out check (Section 03) found the scorer ranks the one known real Schizochytrium colony we have 552nd of 556 — almost dead last — against the environmental candidates. The ranking below should be read as an unvalidated first-pass triage, not a working identification model. See Section 03 for why, and what fixing it requires.

01

The two questions

Both were open at the start; neither was assumed answerable.

Question 1
Given cell-phone photos of 60 environmental sample plates (mixed microbial communities, each shot with the lid on and off), which individual colonies most resemble Schizochytrium, using the lab-maintained reference strains as the visual template?
Question 2
Given photos of the 5 reference strains' own plates, can the strains be told apart from each other by colony appearance — color, size, texture? (The same question already asked and answered for a separate Chlorella sister-strain panel, with a clean negative result there driven by an imaging-batch confound.)
02

Image processing: what actually worked

Three approaches were tried and rejected before landing on the one used for all results below — kept here because the failure modes are informative, not just the final answer.

Finding the dish

Photos are shot on a reflective, scratched stainless-steel bench — not a uniform backdrop — so the background-color-thresholding approach used on the earlier Chlorella panel doesn't transfer. Hough circle detection on the blurred grayscale image finds the dish boundary directly and reliably; the interior is eroded inward (~130px on these ~3000×4000px photos) to exclude the dish's own bright plastic-rim reflection.

Finding colonies: three attempts

Attempt 1 — HSV color threshold (failed)

Worked cleanly for Chlorella (saturated green colonies vs. a neutral blue-gray background) but fails here: the agar itself has a warm cream cast under this lighting, overlapping colony color heavily, and colony saturation itself varied 33–80 across differently-exposed shots of the same strain.

Attempt 2 — global Otsu threshold on brightness (failed)

Handheld phone shots have real illumination gradients across a single dish — one side genuinely brighter than the other, independent of colonies. A single global brightness cutoff per image picks that gradient up as false "growth."

Attempt 3 — local contrast, difference-of-Gaussians (used)

Subtracting a heavily-blurred copy of the brightness channel from itself isolates local elevation — a raised, glossy colony reads as a bright bump regardless of the photo's overall exposure or a smooth lighting gradient across the dish. The one parameter that matters is the blur radius: too small relative to colony size and large colonies fragment into edge-only rings; too large and separate nearby colonies merge into one blob. sigma=250 on these ~3000×4000px images gave the cleanest separation.

Shape and isolation filtering

Reused from the Chlorella pipeline with adjusted thresholds: candidate blobs are kept only if they're plausibly sized, round (circularity > 0.40 — lower than Chlorella's 0.55, since this local-contrast mask has rougher edges than a clean color mask), solid (> 0.78), and — the check that matters most — the crop window around the colony contains no other detected blob. That last check went through two rounds of tuning live with visual QC: an initial 300px² floor for what counts as "another object" let small satellite colonies slip into a crop uncaught (visible in early collages as 2–3 small colonies sharing one frame); lowering it to 100px² and re-running all 60 plates dropped the total from 960 to 555 crops but made single-colony crops the norm rather than the exception.

One plate is a confirmed noise source

Plate 40 has essentially no visible growth, yet produced 1,150 raw "candidates" from sensor/surface-texture noise and its own local-contrast statistics don't distinguish it from genuinely populated plates — it was only caught by directly viewing the crops (pure background, no colony) and the full-plate overlay. It is manually excluded from every ranking below. This is the clearest argument in this whole page for treating automated output as a shortlist for human eyes, not a final answer.

03

Question 1 — Schizochytrium-likeness ranking

Every isolated colony crop, scored against the reference strains, ranked, and spot-checked by eye.

Method

Reference signal is pooled from two sources, since single colonies were scarce on the reference plates themselves (see Question 2): the 1 genuine isolated single-colony crop obtained (strain #7), plus a whole-growth-region color/texture signature computed for all 5 strains (mean/spread of hue, saturation, brightness, plus a texture-roughness measure, over whatever growth — streak, lawn, or colony — is present on each strain's plate). Every environmental colony crop gets the same 7-feature vector, and is scored by its RMS z-score distance to the pooled reference mean. This is a similarity/anomaly score, not a trained or cross-validated classifier — with 6 total reference samples, a real classifier would be fitting noise.

Validation check: this score fails on the one ground-truth example we have

Leave-one-out test: negative

The single genuine isolated colony we do have (strain #7) was scored against a reference distribution built without it — the honest way to ask "would this scorer have recognized a real Schizochytrium colony as Schizochytrium-like?" It ranked 552nd of 556 (99.3rd percentile) against the environmental candidates — the scorer would have placed a known true positive almost dead last.

The likely cause: 5 of the 6 pooled reference samples are whole-lawn/streak-region features, and the one true positive is a single-colony feature — not the same kind of optical measurement. A thick confluent streak smear and an isolated 3D colony dome can differ systematically in color/texture for reasons that have nothing to do with strain identity, and pooling them as interchangeable was a methodological error, not a minor wrinkle. The deeper issue it exposes: there is exactly one single-colony reference sample in existence across all 5 strains — not enough to build a reference distribution at all, let alone a validated one. This ranking should not be treated as a working identification model. The results below are kept for the record and because the visual spot-check (round, smooth, cream-colored colonies) is still a real, human-eye observation independent of the broken score — but the ranking that produced the ordering is not currently trustworthy, and the fix is the same one Question 2 already calls for: real isolated-colony reference plates, imaged the same way as the environmental samples, before this is attempted again.

Result: consistent hotspots, not scattered hits (pre-validation-check framing, see above)

Of the top 60 ranked candidates (plate 40 excluded), most cluster on a handful of plates rather than spreading evenly across all 60 — the strongest signal for "this is picking up something real" in the whole analysis, independent of whether the distance score itself is trustworthy. Plates 47, 56, 7, 52, 17, and 42 account for the majority of strong candidates.

04

Question 2 — can the reference strains be told apart?

Short answer: not with this data, and not close.

This is not a statistically testable question yet

Each of the 5 reference strains is represented by one physical plate (photographed lid-on and lid-off — two photos of the same plate, not two independent samples). With n=1 plate per strain there is no way to estimate within-strain variability at all, so there is no way to tell a real strain difference apart from that specific plate's lighting, growth density, or photo exposure that day. This is a more severe version of the exact confound that produced a clean negative result on the separate Chlorella sister-strain panel (there, at least 2–3 plates per strain existed to directly measure the confound's size).

Worse, 4 of the 5 reference plates (HS6, M42, M64, strain #4) yielded zero cleanly isolated single colonies under the same isolation filter used for the environmental plates — growth was too dense/lawn-like to separate. Only strain #7's plate had a genuinely isolated single colony. Per the user's own observation mid-project: these lab strains were not necessarily streaked under optimal single-colony-isolation conditions.

What can be shown descriptively (not tested)

As a fallback, a whole-growth-region signature (color mean/spread, texture) was computed per strain over whatever growth is present — streak, lawn, or colony. This is a legitimate way to characterize "what this one plate happened to look like," but it is one sample per strain, not a distribution, and should not be read as a strain-vs-strain comparison.

What would actually answer this question

The same fix already prescribed for the Chlorella panel, adapted here: multiple independent plates per reference strain (5–10+), each streaked for genuine single-colony isolation (not lawn growth), imaged with strains interleaved across sessions rather than batched, so within-strain and between-strain variability can both actually be measured. Without that, "can these strains be told apart" stays an open question rather than a negative result — there simply isn't enough data yet to fail the test, let alone pass it.

05

Limitations & evidence tier

Evidence tier: exploratory / crude by design — scoring model failed its own validation check

Everything on this page sits at the lowest confidence tier used across this site, and Question 1's ranking specifically should not be treated as a working model: a leave-one-out check (Section 03) found it ranks the one known real Schizochytrium colony we have 552nd of 556, almost dead last. The pipeline also has at least one confirmed false-positive source (plate 40); isolation quality trades off directly against yield (documented above) and was tuned by visual inspection, not a held-out validation set; and Question 2 is currently unanswerable rather than answered. Treat every image above as a candidate worth a human look — ideally back on the physical plate, or under a microscope — not as a confirmed identification, and treat the ranking order itself with active skepticism given the failed check.

  • The likeness score fails a leave-one-out validation check (Section 03) — traced to pooling whole-lawn/streak-region reference features with a single-colony test feature, two different kinds of optical measurement. With only one true single-colony reference sample in existence, this cannot currently be fixed by better pooling; it needs more single-colony reference data.
  • No molecular confirmation (16S/18S sequencing, microscopy) of any candidate's identity — this is colony-morphology screening only.
  • The reference "distribution" is 6 samples across 5 strains; a properly cross-validated classifier is not appropriate at this sample size (same lesson learned the hard way on the Chlorella panel).
  • Isolation floor (100px²) is a manually chosen trade-off between crop purity and colony yield, not derived from a ground-truth check.
  • Lid-on photos are not used for detection (lid-off only, per the established two-photos-per-plate protocol) but were not separately analyzed for whether lid glare hides any additional colonies.
